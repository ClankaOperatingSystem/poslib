#+TITLE: Simon's scissors

Simon's scissors is Herbert Simon's picture of behaviour as the cut made
by two blades.[fn:1]

The first blade is the structure of the place where work is done, and the
second is the ability of whoever does the work. Simon held that behaviour
cannot be understood from one blade alone.[fn:2] In an AI system the
second blade is the model: its intelligence and instincts push against the
information environment it works in, and what the agent does is decided
where the two meet. poslib works on the first blade: a
[[id:4942c91a-4024-44d5-b549-41a105415fdd][garden]] is an information
environment designed around the physics of personal productivity: Emacs,
[[id:f8f269fe-5e28-49dc-bc73-b9bae96edead][Org matter]], tasks, schedules
and continuous learning.
[[id:86911fc3-3341-49dc-8908-8cb05a92ba23][Grassé's stigmergy]] describes
the two blades in a colony of termites.

[fn:1] Herbert A. Simon, 1916–2001, studied how people and organisations
decide, and won the Nobel prize in economics in 1978:
[[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_A._Simon][Herbert A. Simon]] on
Wikipedia. The picture is from his "Invariants of Human Behavior",
/Annual Review of Psychology/, 1990, where rational behaviour "is shaped
by a scissors whose two blades are the structure of task environments and
the computational capabilities of the actor".

[fn:2] He first argued it in "Rational Choice and the Structure of the
Environment", /Psychological Review/, 1956. See
[[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_rationality][Bounded rationality]]
on Wikipedia.
